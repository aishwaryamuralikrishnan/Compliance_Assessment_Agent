Compliance Assistant demo in nine stages

Start from python scripts/admin.py reset, restart the API (uvicorn api.main:app --reload --port 8000) with the web app running (npm run dev in web/, http://localhost:3000), run one throwaway question to warm up, and keep two windows open that do not share a login: lena.k (product manager) in a normal window and m.weber (DPO) in a private window or a second browser. About 25 minutes.

Plain-words glossary
GDPRThe EU law on handling people's personal data.
AI ActThe EU law on AI systems; some uses (deciding who gets credit) are "high-risk" and carry extra duties.
DPIAA written risk assessment a company must do before starting something risky with personal data.
RegisterThe list every company must keep of what it does with personal data. In this app it is the goal; only the DPO's approval writes to it.
DPOThe data protection officer: the person in the company responsible for all of the above.

We're adding automated credit scoring to our buy-now-pay-later checkout using transaction history, device fingerprint and browsing behaviour. Rejections are fully automatic and scores are shared with our US fraud-prevention vendor. Contact lena.k@example.com if you need details.
 Verdict Do not build as described, high risk. A Blocker: a computer may not make a decision like this about a person with nobody involved (GDPR Art. 22). High-risk AI = yes (it judges creditworthiness, AI Act Annex III point 5). DPIA required = yes. A Required finding on using browsing behaviour, as its own finding (the worker is asked to address every data source the description names). The US data sharing marked Covered (Art. 45) because of the seeded partner fact. Expect several searches. A rewrite only on the Mistral setting: the worker writes its own search queries, and GPT-6 Luna writes them in the law's vocabulary, so the grader accepts the first pass (see the closing note). E-mail blanked out before anything saw it (first trace line). route: full. While it runs: the progress stepper and, in Behind the scenes, the worker's retrieve → grade boxes and the search_regulation blocks in the trace (retrieve → grade: sufficient → stop; on the Mistral setting also grade: insufficient → rewrite_query → retrieve 2). Mention what did not show on the happy path: reject (the third decision), doom-loop exit, thread summarisation, citation regeneration.

Model output varies a little between runs. If in stage 5 every finding is already a Blocker, reword one finding instead; the lesson still appears. Rehearse stages 3, 5 and 7 once before presenting: stage 3 should produce the Covered finding, a Required one and at least one query rewrite, and stage 7 should return the Blocker. Whether a rewrite appears depends on the worker, not on how the request is worded: the worker writes its own search queries (one focused topic per call, in the law's terms), and the grader sends a query back only when the passages miss the provision. Measured on 23 Oct with scripts/probe_grader.py (Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite as grader): 6 of 6 plain-English queries were sent back and rewritten, 4 of 4 precise ones accepted on the first pass, so the loop works as designed. GPT-6 Luna writes precise queries and gets no rewrite; Mistral Large 4 wrote looser ones and the stage-3 wording gave two. If the rewrite matters for the presentation, present on the Mistral setting (config/models.yaml keeps both; swap the comment lines in the two worker slots) and rehearse once with it; otherwise skip the rewrite in stage 3 and show the loop from the terminal if asked.
